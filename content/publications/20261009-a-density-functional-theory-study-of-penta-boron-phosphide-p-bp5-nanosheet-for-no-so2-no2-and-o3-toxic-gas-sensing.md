In this study, we investigated the gas-sensing performance of a Penta-boron phosphide (P-BP5) nanosheet toward NO, SO2, NO2, and O3 using density functional theory (DFT) calculations. The phonon spectra and molecular dynamics simulations confirmed dynamical and thermodynamical stability, with a cohesive energy of -5.692 eV and a direct band gap of 1.857 eV. Among all gases, NO2 had a significant adsorption energy of -3.583 eV at an adsorption distance of 2.754 Å, as well as better gas sensing and chemisorption abilities. The recovery times ranged from 0.792 × 10-9 s to 3.99 × 1048 s, demonstrating a high attractive energy for NO2 and O3. After gas adsorption, the band gap changes significantly. Adsorption of NO and NO2 on P-BP5 reduces the band gap to zero, significantly enhancing conductivity and sensitivity at room temperature. Work function analysis revealed larger fluctuations for NO, demonstrating the efficacy of work function-based gas sensors.
