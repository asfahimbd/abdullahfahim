We performed classical molecular dynamics simulations using Large-scale Atomic/Massively Parallel Simulator (LAMMPS) to investigate hydrogen implantation, retention, diffusion, and radiation damage in a Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) tungsten slab. We explored a parameter space comprising ten implantation energies (5–300 eV) and ten substrate temperatures (300 1500 K), with 1,000 independent simulations and ten statistical repeats per condition. Hydrogen retention exhibits a pronounced non-monotonic dependence on implantation energy, peaking at 70 eV (fret = 0.886 ± 0.031) and declining sharply at both sub-threshold energies — where surface reflection dominates — and at energies above 150 eV, where deep penetration and cascade-enhanced recombination reduce net retention. The mean implantation range increases from 6 Å at 5 eV to a saturation plateau near 43–45 Å above 70 eV. Arrhenius analysis of the directional diffusion coefficient Dz yielded activation energies in the range of 0.12–0.25 eV and pre-exponential factors of 6.8 × 10−8 to 1.2 × 10−6 m2 s−1 across implantation energies of 5–150 eV. Implantation energies of 200 and 300 eV were identified as belonging to a cascade dominated regime, in which thermal spikes reaching ∼15,400 K were found to invalidate the Arrhenius diffusion model. Tungsten radiation damage, quantified as displaced atoms above a 300 K thermal baseline, was found to remain modest at temperatures up to 1233 K for most implantation energies; however, it rises sharply at T ≥ 1366 K. These results provide a statistically resolved, parameter-sweeping dataset of H–W interaction parameters for informing continuum transport models and rate-theory descriptions used in the design of fusion plasma facing materials
