### Abstract

Computationally intensive SRIM (Stopping and Range of Ions in Matter) simulations are used to design and optimize advanced semiconductor materials, including silicon (Si), silicon carbide (SiC), and gallium arsenide (GaAs), to be used in photovoltaic (PV) solar cells and electric vehicle (EV) power devices. These simulations are physically accurate but require substantial computational resources and time, thus forming a key bottleneck in the fast development of next-generation energy materials. To significantly address this shortcoming, a hybrid Random Forest-based surrogate model has been developed to effectively replicate the results of SRIM ion implantation across a wide range of conditions. The scheme works in two consecutive stages: firstly, a classifier separates zero-range and profile events with a total accuracy of 91%; secondly, a multi-regressor model predicts nine critical physical parameters, such as projected range, straggle, lateral spread, vacancy concentration, backscattered ions, and transmitted ions.

Approximately 5,600 validated SRIM simulations, including a wide range of ion species (Ar, As, B, Mg, P) and a variety of implantation energies, angles, and substrate thicknesses of Si, SiC, and GaAs targets, were used to train the surrogate model. The coefficient of determination (R²) was obtained at an average of 0.97, demonstrating a high level of predictive power. Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) analysis confirmed that the Random Forest model achieved an error of only \~275 Å for projected range prediction, ensuring physical consistency with SRIM-simulated outputs.

By providing real-time and accurate predictions, the surrogate model reduces the need for extensive SRIM simulations, which impose a significant computational and energy cost. The development enables rapid optimization of implantation parameters to achieve accurate doping, defect minimization, and junction optimization during solar cell fabrication, as well as the development of high-performance SiC-based EV power electronics. The framework, therefore, provides a scalable and energy-efficient solution that accelerates innovation in the power and energy materials sector.
