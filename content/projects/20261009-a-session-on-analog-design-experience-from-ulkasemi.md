description: |-
  I took a session on analog integrated circuit (IC) design after an intensive 5 days training from Ulkasemi. During the session, I provided a comprehensive overview of the field, emphasizing that despite the digital nature of modern computing, analog circuits remain essential for processing real-world continuous signals (like human voice) through components such as ADCs, DACs, and amplifiers. I explained the standard analog design workflow, transitioning from initial conceptualization and schematic implementation to rigorous simulation, physical layout, and physical verification (such as LVS and DRC) before parasitic extraction and final sign-off. A significant portion of my technical discussion focused on MOSFET operations—specifically the cutoff, linear, and saturation regions—and how phenomena like channel-length modulation (pinch-off) can disrupt ideal current flow. To mitigate these issues, I detailed how engineers utilize current mirror circuits to maintain a constant bias current, typically adjusting the transistor's channel width rather than its length to meet specific technology node constraints without causing resistance mismatches.

  Transitioning to industry practices, I highlighted Cadence Virtuoso as the premier EDA tool for analog design, though I noted that university students often build their foundational skills using accessible software like ModelSim. I outlined the recruitment pipeline at top VLSI firms like Ulkasemi, which typically involves online and written tests, an interview, a selective training bootcamp, and a final assessment that can lead to a trainee engineering role. Beyond core technical knowledge in solid-state physics, electronics, and programming (Verilog, C++, Python), I heavily stressed the importance of soft skills like leadership and teamwork, which can be cultivated through university clubs like IEEE. Finally, I reassured the audience about the career outlook in this field; because analog design involves a massive number of continuous variables, I explained that it is currently highly resistant to AI automation, making it a secure and highly promising engineering path.
